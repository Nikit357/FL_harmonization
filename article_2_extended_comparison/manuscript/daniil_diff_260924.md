The problem recurs whenever profiles generated by different assays are analysed together, as in retroelement-linked epigenomic comparisons [23,24,25,26,27,28].

− The machine-learning literature states what follows when the held-out test is chosen carelessly. A cross-validation performance estimate is biased when the batch variable is confounded with the class labels, and removing the batch effect does not remove that bias [29]. When batch and outcome are correlated, a predictor can score well inside one study and generalize poorly out of sample [30]. A protocol that selects a model and then reports its score under the same protocol is internally inconsistent, and the inconsistency favours the procedures most prone to over-fitting the selection criterion [31]. The minimum cross-validated error reached by tuning is itself biased downwards [32], feature selection performed on the same samples adds a further bias [33], and the unit a cross-validation splits on has to match the generalization being claimed, shown for splits made by subject against splits made by record [34]; we apply that principle to batches and cut our folds by batch. A relationship introduced by pre-processing inflates measured performance [35]. The structure of genomics data can itself bias a performance evaluation [36], and the fold-level spread carries no unbiased variance estimator, so a difference between two cross-validated scores cannot be read off it [37].

The clustermap group averaged 37.9% and 11.6% of total variance and 22.9% and 15.2% batch R2 on the first two components; 14_qsmooth, the weakest method on the PCA metrics, averaged 53.7% and 13.5% of variance with 90.8% and 67.1% batch R2; the unharmonized data averaged 73.5% and 9.7% of variance with 64.8% and 65.9% batch R2. The RNA-seq-only strategy and the clustermap group showed the lowest first-component variance (30.4% and 37.9%) among the batch removal strategies and the smoothest decay (Supplementary Figure 2F), whereas the Affymetrix-only strategy had the highest variance explained by the first component (66.0%). The individual principal components analysis therefore showed that harmonization benchmarking based on the first component only inherently underestimates batch effect, and harmonization methods can hide the batch-related variance into the lower rank principal components which should be accounted for during benchmarking.  `[C35]`

+ Local neighborhood metrics discriminate well between good and bad approaches but are vulnerable to batch composition

+ Only six of the 31 methods (02_median_scaling, 10_mnn, 16_fsqn_r, 25_angel, 28_npn and 33_amdbnorm) mixed batches better than biology by LISI (Supplementary Figure 4A). The kBET acceptance rate behaved as more stringent criterion: it stayed under 0.01 for 82.0% of the 2,234 approaches and under 0.10 for 97.0% (Supplementary Figure 5A). Five of the 15 clustermap best approaches exceeded 0.10, and these approaches were SVA under the RNA-seq-only and FFPE-only strategies. Eight methods exceeded the unharmonized baseline of 0.027 by batch kBET acceptance rate: 22_tmm at 0.331 (RNA-seq only), 23_vst at 0.230 (RNA-seq only), 10_mnn at 0.044, 04_sva at 0.036, 36_explobatch at 0.036, 13_fsmvn at 0.035, 20_shambhala at 0.029 and 19_tdm at 0.028. The other 22 methods scored below raw dataset, indicating that they do not improve local batch mixing (Supplementary Figure 5C).

+ According to their design, local values depended on how many classes the strategy leaves in the data, which limits comparison across strategies. The malignant-only and FFPE-only strategies retained four malignant groups and returned the lowest biology LISI (medians 1.10 and 1.07), whereas the FF-only strategy preserved all the normal B-cell types and returned the highest value (median 1.62, Supplementary Figure 4B). The same two strategies (FFPE-only, malignant-only) returned the lowest biology graph connectivity (0.646 and 0.727 against 0.799 to 0.854 for the rest, Supplementary Figure 7D), although fewer biology groups are expected to increase connectivity, which could derive from their assembly from heterogeneous FFPE batches. The RNA-seq-only strategy produced a tight group of 41 approaches spanning 18 methods, with kBET between 0.654 and 0.867, and none of them was selected by the clustermap, because their LISI values stayed between 1.24 and 1.38 (Supplementary Figure 5B).  `[C37,38]`

+ Taking together, the in-depth analysis of local metrics showed that reaching the maximum of one local metric is not sufficient for the clustermap best selection. Moreover, the local metrics were highly dependent on prior batch removal strategies. These findings indicate that a robust harmonization quality benchmarking pipeline should treat local metrics as necessary but not sufficient metric family.

+ Distributional convergence is the only axis on which the high harshness harmonization methods rank first

+ We further deeply compared metrics of distributional similarity between harmonization approaches. On the fraction of cohort pairs whose expression distributions differ by the KS test, 29 of the 31 methods had the score below the unharmonized baseline of 0.948, and only 27_dwd was worse than the raw expression mnatrix (0.965, Supplementary Figure 7A). Only ten methods brought the metric below 0.7, ranging from 0.173 for 28_npn and 0.217 for 26_xpn up to 0.691 for 03_limma, and the clustermap best group reached 0.560, so 56.0% of cohort pairs still differed after the best harmonization by the clustermap assessment. Distance ratio separated the clustermap group as well (batch distance ratio between 0.845 and 1.032, with a median of 0.914 against 0.757 over the full set, Supplementary Figure 7E), with the only the two methods, 27_dwd (0.508) and 14_qsmooth (0.531), having the metric below the unharmonized dataset (0.566). The Watermelon score did not separate the clustermap group from the rest approaches (Supplementary Figure 7G to 7J), which makes this metric a candidate for an independent metric class to be compared to the global and local families.  `[C40,41]`

+ We then compared methods by their harshness score defined in the harmonization benchmark (Nikitin et al. 2026 Sep 17) as low harshness is linear transformation, high harshness is significant distribution reshaping (for example in quantile normalization) and medium harshness is everything in between. There were 344 low, 1,042 medium and 848 high harshness approaches for the comparison. Low-harshness methods demonstrated the highest batch PCReg under 11 strategies of 14, and the highest batch LISI under 10 of 14, while high-harshness methods showed lowest tSNE entropy under 12 strategies of 14 (Supplementary Figure 8A, 8C, 8D). High harshness prevailed over low and middle harshness on one axis only, namely distributional convergence, where it demonstrated the lowest KS fraction significant under 13 strategies of 14 (Supplementary Figure 8F). Matching expression distributions to a same distribution is a distinguishing feature of harshest transformations, but it does not lead to improvement of local or global metrics.

+ We also calculated and compared a composite score, which was the equally weighted composite of all 87 metrics. Expectedly, it ranked the clustermap group as first at 0.602, with the RNA-seq-only strategy next at the level of 0.578 (Supplementary Figure 9A). The composite score was dominated by global distance metrics, that supply 47 of the 87 columns and 0.360 of the group’s score while varying by only 0.017 across strategies and 0.030 across methods (Supplementary Figure 9A, B, respectively). In contrast, local neighborhood metrics provide only 22 columns and 0.109 of the score but produce the largest between-group spreads, 0.043 across strategies and 0.049 across methods for the neighbor-based integration group (Supplementary Figure 9C, 9D, respectively). Batch-annotated columns supply 57 metrics of the 87 and between 60.7 and 67.4% of the composite score, and the 18 biology columns separate approaches weakly: across strategies their spread is 0.053, and for the batch columns it is 0.122 (Supplementary Figure 9E, 9F, respectively).  `[C42,43]`

+ We also sorted all approaches by the composite score and revealed an upper tail of 150 approaches above 0.6 (6.7% of total appricase), containing 9 of the 15 clustermap selections (Supplementary Figure 10). We did not reveal ordering of the harshness tiers by this composite score.

+ We then compared the biology related metrics of classes L, M and N between the harshness groups and observed clear differences (Supplementary Figure 12). Low-harshness methods showed the mean biomarker correlation before and after harmonization at 0.998 against 0.880 for medium and 0.922 for high (Kruskal-Wallis H = 340.1, p = 1.4 x 10-74, n = 344 / 1,042 / 848), and had the smallest marker-minus-housekeeping contrast (-0.000 against 0.009 and 0.031; H = 139.3, p = 5.5 x 10-31, n = 344 / 1,042 / 800). High-harshness methods destroyed cross-batch rank agreement (0.507 against 0.691 and 0.701; H = 287.1, p = 4.5 x 10-63, n = 344 / 1,042 / 847) and carried the smallest margin (0.030 against 0.054 and 0.045; H = 132.9, p = 1.4 x 10-29, same n). On the full-cut LOBO three-class F1 the low tier was last (0.631 against 0.712 and 0.715; H = 51.6, p = 6.1 x 10-12, n = 344 / 1,042 / 847); on the multiclass-only cut the three tiers spanned 0.021 (0.657, 0.678, 0.678; H = 11.8, p = 2.8 x 10-3, n = 333 / 1,016 / 825); and the two-class AUC did not separate them (0.901, 0.904, 0.904; H = 1.5, p = 0.48, same n). An earlier calculation of the multiclass-only test over the unrestricted fold table gave H = 11.2 and p = 0.0037, and an earlier reading of the medium-tier full-cut median gave 0.713; the values quoted here, H = 11.8, p = 2.8 x 10-3 and 0.712, come from the fold population restricted to the analysis set and from the table at three decimals, and the tier ordering is the same under both.  `[C44,45,46,47]`

+ Taken together, we showed that prediction quality metrics (class N) are better in medium and high harshness approaches, whereas cross batch agreement by biomarker genes is decreases in high harshness. This opposite trend could indicate tradeoff between biology subtype prediction and biomarker correlation preservation metrics.  `[C48]`

+ Preservation of biomarker expression and of cross-batch rank structure selects a distinct set of approaches

+ Since none of the 87 scoring metrics in the published benchmark assess expression of biomarker genes, we deeply studied performance of L and M metric classes. These classes were visualized for the clustermap best approach in the benchmarking article but there were not extensively compared between batch removal strategies and harmonization methods. Plotting raw against harmonized expression, gene by gene and cohort by cohort, shows what each class is reacting to: the unharmonized baseline lies on the identity line by construction (Figure 4A), 13_fsmvn moves every point along a per-gene affine path and keeps the within-cohort order (Figure 4B), 12_scanorama returns a cloud with no relation to the input (Supplementary Figure 14A), and 29_combat_ref shifts the cohorts onto a common scale while keeping the within-cohort spread (Supplementary Figure 14B).  `[C50]`
