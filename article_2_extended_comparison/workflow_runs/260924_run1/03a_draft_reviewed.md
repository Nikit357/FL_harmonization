### P8 [edit]
Methods. We used the 2,234 approaches of the recent real-world benchmark on 7,174 B-cell lymphoma transcriptomes. They combine 31 harmonization methods, 14 batch-removal strategies, three imputations and two post-removal settings. We assigned its 87 polarity-adjusted scoring metrics, out of 344 registered, to five classes (global, local, distributional, structural and composite) and added three biology-facing classes: within-cohort marker correlation (L), cross-batch rank agreement of a marker panel (M), and leave-one-batch-out (LOBO) prediction of lymphoma subtypes and normal B cells (N). ### P13 [edit]
Each benchmark of these algorithms declares a different criterion of success. An early 2011 empirical comparison of cross-platform normalization scored methods on inter-platform concordance of gene lists supported by ROC-like curves and bootstrap ⟦CIT:(Rudy and Valafar 2011)⟧. A later comparison scored the methods by the accuracy of a downstream subtype classifier ⟦CIT:(Thompson et al. 2016)⟧. The single-cell integration literature supplied a third criterion, a trade-off between batch effect removal and biology conservation ⟦CIT:(Luecken et al. 2021)⟧. A benchmark of batch-effect correction methods for single-cell RNA sequencing recorded the same trade-off, reporting also that quality metrics can disagree with visual inspection of the embeddings ⟦CIT:(Tran et al. 2020)⟧. Two of the metrics that this literature contributed, the k-nearest-neighbor (KNN) batch-effect test ⟦CIT:(Büttner et al. 2018)⟧  and the local inverse Simpson’s index ⟦CIT:(Korsunsky et al. 2019)⟧, are now applied to bulk data, albeit there is still no standard of harmonization quality measurement ⟦CIT:(Borisov and Buzdin 2022)⟧.

### P14 [edit]
The harmonization quality criteria are especially important in datasets where the biological contrast is small compared to the batch level technical differences. In the case of germinal center (GC) B-cell lymphomas, expression profiling splits diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) into germinal-center and activated B-cell types ⟦CIT:(Alizadeh et al. 2000)⟧ predicting survival after chemotherapy ⟦CIT:(Rosenwald et al. 2002)⟧. Survival in follicular lymphoma (FL) is predicted by subtle differences in immune-cell signatures of the microenvironment ⟦CIT:(Dave et al. 2004)⟧, and a 23-gene score separates FL patients into two outcome groups under immunochemotherapy ⟦CIT:(Huet et al. 2018)⟧. These small-scale transcriptomic differences are challenging to survive transfer between materials and platforms, explaining the cell-of-origin assignment reinvention for formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue ⟦CIT:(Scott et al. 2014)⟧ and multiple cohort sources for genetic subtypes of DLBCL ⟦CIT:(Chapuy et al. 2018; Schmitz et al. 2018)⟧. The same transfer problem governs expression signatures outside lymphoma, for example microsatellite instability status ⟦CIT:(Sorokin et al. 2021)⟧, therapy prescription in colorectal ⟦CIT:(Sorokin et al. 2022)⟧ and kidney cancer ⟦CIT:(Gudkov et al. 2022)⟧ and DNA repair pathway activation in thyroid tumors ⟦CIT:(Vladimirova et al. 2021)⟧. In a recent T-cell lymphoma xenograft study, principal-component analysis (PCA) showed only a partial overlap between primary tumors and their matched xenografts, which the authors attributed to tumor content and host cells, and that variation was removed by surrogate variable analysis (SVA) ⟦CIT:(Fiore et al. 2025)⟧.

### P15 [edit]
In case of the real-world batch correction scenario, the analysis is complicated by confounding between the batch variable and with the class labels ⟦CIT:(Soneson et al. 2014)⟧ which drastically reduces predictors generalizability ⟦CIT:(Parker and Leek 2012)⟧. Moreover, cross-validation could be biased under these scenarios ⟦CIT:(Varma and Simon 2006)⟧ which is amplified by selection bias among thousands of genes in transcriptomic datasets containing only tens to hundreds of samples ⟦CIT:(Ambroise and McLachlan 2002)⟧. These issues lead to prediction failures of models deployed in clinical practice ⟦CIT:(Saeb et al. 2017; Whalen et al. 2021)⟧, described as the ‘reproducibility crisis’ in the machine learning science in general ⟦CIT:(Kapoor and Narayanan 2023)⟧ .

### P21 [edit]

### P24 [edit]
Within a class we converted every metric to a rank percentile over the 2,234 approaches, oriented so that a higher percentile is better according to the polarity assignments from the benchmark ⟦CIT:(Nikitin et al. 2026 Sep 17)⟧, and averaged the percentiles into one class score per approach (Supplementary File 1, sheet Class_percentile_scores). For each class we took top 5% approaches as the best ones (112 approaches in total). For comparison, we treated the 15 approaches selected by the published clustermap ⟦CIT:(Nikitin et al. 2026 Sep 17)⟧ as one more set. Overlap between each two elected sets was calculated as Jaccard index.

### P29 [edit]
Class N metrics were designed as leave-one-batch-out prediction to measure a transferability and generalizability of the best harmonization approaches selected. For each approach and each of the 24 RNA batches we excluded this batch and fitted logistic regression in principal components on the training batches alone for the three-class target (DLBCL, FL and normal B cells) and for the two-class target (FL against DLBCL). Then we projected the PCA coordinates at the held-out batch and predicted its biology labels using the previously fitted logistic regression, calculating Area Under Curve (AUC) and F1 scores. We also controlled this calculation using 100 biological label permutations and averaged all the N class metrics separately on multiclass batches (having more than one biology class) and on all batches. Multiclass-only batches were selected in order to remove a potential F1/AUC bias in batches that contain single biology group. The AUC is undefined on a held-out batch with a single biology group, so every AUC mean is a mean over multiclass batches only.

### P31 [edit]
Group comparisons across three or more groups used the Kruskal-Wallis H test, with ε² as its effect size, and monotone associations used the Spearman correlation coefficient. Two groups were compared by the two-sided Mann-Whitney U test, and matched pairs of approaches, which share strategy, method and the remaining pipeline settings and differ in one factor only, by the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Proportions were compared between two groups by the two-sided Fisher's exact test and against their distribution in the analysis set by the chi-square goodness-of-fit test or the two-sided binomial test. The number of strategies in which one harshness tier ranked first was compared with the one-in-three chance level by the two-sided binomial test, and a count of strategies with a lower value on one principal component than on the next by the two-sided binomial sign test. The overlap of two elected sets was compared with the overlap expected for two random sets of the same sizes by the hypergeometric distribution, reported as the probability of an overlap at least as large, or at least as small, as observed. All p-values are raw, without correction for multiple testing. Per-strategy coefficients were analyzed as their ranges and medians and selected groups were reported by their medians and median absolute deviations (MAD); the spread of group means is reported as their range, the maximum minus the minimum. We ran the analyses under Python 3.11 with pandas 2.3.3, numpy 1.26.4, scipy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.8.0, statsmodels 0.14.6, matplotlib 3.10.8 and seaborn 0.13.2.

### P76+1 [insert]
Taken together, the analysis of classes L and M showed that marker correlation saturates for the methods that change expression least, and that cross-batch agreement is highest for the methods that collapse expression onto a constant profile, so neither class can select the best approaches alone. A harmonization benchmarking pipeline should use marker correlation as a gate, read cross-batch agreement through the same-minus-different-biology margin relative to the unharmonized baseline, and check the scale of the harmonized output separately.
