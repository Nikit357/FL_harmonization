# Metric classes elect non-overlapping sets of harmonization approaches: a cross-election analysis of 2,234 approaches in a multi-platform B-cell lymphoma benchmark

Daniil Nikitin^1,2^*, Nikolay Borisov^3^, Maria Savchenko^2^, Anatoly Bobe^2^, Mark Meerson^2^, Alexander Nesmelov^2^, Nazar Harutyunyan^2^, Svetlana Paponova^2^, Andrey Kravets^2^, Alexandr Zaitsev^2^, Alexandr Bagaev^2^ and Arsen Arakelyan^1^

^1^Institute of Molecular Biology, National Academy of Science of the Republic of Armenia, Yerevan, 0014, Armenia
^2^[affiliation 2: as in the published article]
^3^Armenian Bioinformatics Institute, Yerevan, 0014, Armenia

*Corresponding author. Email: danya.nikitin.orel@gmail.com

**Keywords:** batch effect, transcriptomic harmonization, benchmarking metrics, cross-validation bias, leave-one-batch-out prediction, follicular lymphoma, gene expression

## Abstract

**Background.** Benchmarks of cross-platform transcriptomic harmonization each declare the criterion by which they judge success: correlation with a reference profile, classifier accuracy, or a composite imported from single-cell integration. We found no published study that computes several metric classes over one set of harmonization approaches and measures how far their selections overlap.

**Methods.** We used the 2,234 approaches of one benchmark: 31 harmonization methods, 14 batch-removal strategies, 3 imputations and 2 post-removal settings on 7,174 B-cell lymphoma transcriptomes. We assigned its 87 polarity-adjusted metrics to five classes (global, local, distributional, structural, and their composite) and added three biology-facing classes: within-cohort marker correlation (L), cross-batch rank agreement of a marker panel (M), and leave-one-batch-out (LOBO) prediction of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma and normal B cells (N). Each class elected its top 112 approaches (5%) by its own rank-percentile composite, and we measured overlap by the Jaccard index. Every LOBO value is reported on all folds and on multiclass folds.

**Results.** The largest overlap between any two of the eight elected sets was 0.333 (local against composite, 56 shared approaches). Classes L and M shared no approach, M and N none, L and N one. The 15 approaches selected by the published clustermap shared none of the 112 that class N elected. Class N elected the ComBat family in 84 of its 112 places, class L elected unharmonized or near-unharmonized output in 93, and class M elected two methods whose median marker correlation was -0.0007 and -0.0082. Of the 32,746 three-class LOBO folds, 15,436 (47.1%) carried one class and reached a median macro F1 of 0.903, against 0.7334 on the rest.

**Conclusions.** A benchmark that reports one metric class reports one election. We give a six-step recipe that states the metric class, the gate and the held-out test before ranking.

## Introduction

Technical variation between sequencing platforms, RNA extraction protocols and tissue preservation methods separates samples of the same biological class, and it distorts inference whenever it is correlated with the outcome under study [1]. Algorithms that remove this variation from bulk expression matrices have been catalogued for microarray integration [2] and reviewed for the cross-platform case [3].

Each benchmark of these algorithms declares a criterion of success, and the criteria differ. An empirical comparison of cross-platform normalization scored methods on inter-platform concordance together with over-detection and under-detection, and stated that neither detection statistic ranks the methods on its own [4]. A later comparison scored the same family of methods by the accuracy of a downstream subtype classifier [5]. The single-cell integration literature supplied a third criterion, a composite of batch-correction and biological-conservation metrics, and reported that named methods favour one half of that composite at the expense of the other [6]. A benchmark of batch-effect correction methods for single-cell RNA sequencing recorded the same phenomenon in its own results, finding that its assessment metrics did not always agree with each other or with visual inspection of the embeddings [7]. Two of the metrics that this literature contributed, the k-nearest-neighbour batch-effect test [8] and the local inverse Simpson's index [9], are now applied to bulk data far outside the setting for which they were designed. The cross-platform harmonization review sets these criteria side by side and records no agreed standard among them [3].

The criterion matters most where the biological contrast is small. Expression profiling splits diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) into germinal-centre and activated B-cell types [10], those types predict survival after chemotherapy [11], survival in follicular lymphoma (FL) is predicted by immune-cell signatures of the microenvironment [12], and a 23-gene score separates FL patients into two outcome groups under immunochemotherapy [13]. Each of these readouts has to survive transfer between materials and platforms, which is why cell-of-origin assignment was rebuilt for formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue [14], and why genetic subtypes of DLBCL are defined on cohorts assembled from several sources [15,16]. The same transfer problem governs expression signatures outside lymphoma, including microsatellite instability status [17], therapy prescription in colorectal cancer [18], sorafenib response in kidney cancer [19], DNA repair pathway activation in thyroid tumours [20] and endometriosis [21]. In a T-cell lymphoma xenograft resource, principal-component analysis showed only a partial overlap between primary tumours and their matched xenografts, which the authors attributed to tumour content and host cells, and that variation was removed by surrogate variable analysis before the profiles were compared [22]. The problem recurs whenever profiles generated by different assays are analysed together, as in retroelement-linked epigenomic comparisons [23,24,25,26,27,28].

The machine-learning literature states what follows when the held-out test is chosen carelessly. A cross-validation performance estimate is biased when the batch variable is confounded with the class labels, and removing the batch effect does not remove that bias [29]. When batch and outcome are correlated, a predictor can score well inside one study and generalize poorly out of sample [30]. A protocol that selects a model and then reports its score under the same protocol is internally inconsistent, and the inconsistency favours the procedures most prone to over-fitting the selection criterion [31]. The minimum cross-validated error reached by tuning is itself biased downwards [32], feature selection performed on the same samples adds a further bias [33], and the unit a cross-validation splits on has to match the generalization being claimed, shown for splits made by subject against splits made by record [34]; we apply that principle to batches and cut our folds by batch. A relationship introduced by pre-processing inflates measured performance [35]. The structure of genomics data can itself bias a performance evaluation [36], and the fold-level spread carries no unbiased variance estimator, so a difference between two cross-validated scores cannot be read off it [37].

We took the 2,234 harmonization approaches of a published multi-platform B-cell lymphoma benchmark [38], which scored 87 polarity-adjusted metrics across 31 methods, 14 prior batch-removal strategies, 3 imputation schemes and 2 post-removal settings, and inverted its procedure. Instead of asking which approach the full metric set ranks first, we asked which approaches each metric class selects when it is allowed to choose alone, and how far those choices intersect. We added three biology-facing metric classes that the published composite did not contain, covering marker-gene correlation, cross-batch rank structure and leave-one-batch-out prediction, and we report every prediction value on two cuts of the fold population so that the effect of single-class held-out batches is visible in the same table as the value it affects. The result is an election, run eight times on the same 2,234 candidates, and a recipe for the next benchmark.

## Materials and methods

### Dataset, harmonization approaches and the analysis set

The dataset, the 14 prior batch-removal strategies, the 3 imputation schemes, the harmonization method registry and the optional post-removal step are specified in the benchmark that produced them [38]: 7,174 bulk transcriptomes from 88 germinal-centre B-cell lymphoma cohorts across RNA sequencing and Affymetrix, Illumina and Agilent microarrays. We re-used that output and computed no new harmonization. The algorithms named repeatedly below are ComBat with its reference and count-based variants [39,40], surrogate variable analysis [41], mutual nearest neighbours [42], feature-specific quantile normalization [43], cross-platform normalization by XPN [44] and Shambhala [45,46]; the full registry with the parameters used is given in the source benchmark [38].

An approach is one combination of strategy, imputation, harmonization method and post-removal setting. Our analysis set is the canonical set of the source benchmark, defined there by the condition that fewer than 5% of samples carry no defined expression value at all (`pct_samples_allNA < 5`). It holds 2,234 approaches over 31 methods, 14 strategies, 3 imputations and both post-removal settings. Two methods, 34_arsyn and 38_harman, have a minimum of 74.23% of samples with no defined expression across all 84 of their approaches each, so no approach of either method enters the set, which is how the registry of 33 implemented methods reduces to 31.

Shambhala-2 [46] carries two names in the source tables. The metric snapshot stores its 18 parameter variants under names of the form `shambhala_P0std_Q0std`, whereas the benchmark kept one canonical representative and recorded it as `20_shambhala`; the 84 rows of the canonical variant and the 84 rows of `20_shambhala` share all 84 strategy, imputation and post-removal keys and agree on 78 of their 79 shared numeric columns, the single exception being wall-clock compute time (Supplementary File 1, sheet `Canonical_set_reconciliation`). We therefore renamed the canonical variant to `20_shambhala` and dropped the other 17 variants as the first operation of every table load, before any filter or count. Omitting that rename returns 2,150 approaches and silently loses 84.

### Metric classes and the election procedure

We assigned each of the 87 polarity-adjusted scoring metrics of the source benchmark to one of four classes by its column prefix: global, covering principal component regression (PCReg), the dispersion separability criterion (DSC), per-component R^2^ and the variance explained by each principal component; local, covering the k-nearest-neighbour batch-effect test (kBET), the integration and cell-type local inverse Simpson's indices (iLISI and cLISI), average silhouette width and graph connectivity; distributional, covering the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) statistics and per-gene batch quantities; and structural, covering tSNE and UMAP embedding statistics, intra- and inter-group distances, the distance ratio and the Watermelon score. A fifth class, the composite, is the equally weighted mean of those four.

Within a class we converted every metric to a rank percentile over the 2,234 approaches, oriented so that a higher percentile is better, and averaged the percentiles into one class score per approach. The class then elects its top 5%, which is 112 approaches. We treated the 15 approaches selected by the published clustermap [38] as a ninth named group, carried through the same matrix without a score of its own, so the classes can be compared against the published choice. Overlap between two elected sets is their Jaccard index, and the intersection counts quoted in the text are recovered from the Jaccard index and the two set sizes.

We also built a generalizability index as the mean of six rank percentiles: the full-cut and multiclass-only LOBO three-class macro F1, the two-class LOBO macro area under the curve (AUC), the mean within-cohort marker correlation, the Group M margin gain over the unharmonized baseline, and the worst multiclass fold. Rank percentiles were used because the six columns carry incompatible scales and because marker correlation saturates at 1.0 by construction for any monotone per-batch transform.

Because every comparison in this work is run over the full analysis set, we compare batch-removal strategies against each other, imputations against each other and harmonization methods against each other, with the clustermap-selected group carried through as one isolated named group. We do not repeat the two-group test between that group and the remaining approaches, which the source benchmark already reports [38].

### The biology-facing metric classes L, M and N

Class L measures, for each cohort and each gene of a 633-gene marker panel assembled from published FL and DLBCL studies [38], the Spearman correlation of that gene's expression before and after harmonization, together with the contrast against a housekeeping control [47]. The panel and its 56-gene quality-controlled subset are described in the source benchmark [38]. Earlier expression work shows the two readouts the panel combines: a two-gene signature that predicts a phenotype better than either of its genes alone [48], and individual markers validated across tumour grades at transcript and protein level [49]. We treated a mean marker correlation above 0.75 as a necessary condition. Passing it carries no information on its own, because a harmonizer applying a per-batch monotone transform of each gene leaves within-cohort ranks unchanged and scores near 1.0 by construction. Class L therefore functions as a gate, and the ordering is carried by classes M and N.

Class M measures, within a single harmonized matrix, the Spearman rank agreement of the marker panel between pairs of batches, computed separately for pairs of the same biological group and for pairs of different groups. The margin is the same-biology agreement minus the different-biology agreement. Because agreement alone rises whenever an algorithm converges all profiles, we also report the margin relative to the unharmonized `01_raw` baseline at the same strategy and imputation, joined on that pair, and we name approaches that raise same-biology agreement and lower different-biology agreement against their own baseline as agreement-specific.

Class N is leave-one-batch-out prediction. For each approach and each of the 24 RNA batches in turn, we fitted principal components on the training batches alone, projected the held-out batch, fitted a logistic regression on the training projections and predicted the held-out batch, for the three-class target (DLBCL, FL and normal B cells) and for the two-class target (FL against DLBCL), with a permutation null over 100 label permutations. Fitting the projection on training batches only is what makes the held-out batch a transfer test [34], and cutting folds by batch is what the confounding results require [29,30].

Every Class N value is reported on two cuts of the same fold population, and neither replaces the other. The full cut averages over all folds. The multiclass-only cut averages over folds carrying at least two classes. A single-class fold is a test in its own right: a classifier trained on every batch but one can still mislabel a DLBCL-only batch, and dropping such folds would remove that failure from the record. Every prediction table below therefore carries four columns together, the full mean, the multiclass-only mean, the worst multiclass fold and the multiclass fold count.

### Statistics and software

Group comparisons across three or more groups used the Kruskal-Wallis H test, and monotone associations used the Spearman correlation coefficient. These two are the only inferential procedures in `analysis/article2_generalizability.py`: we compute no confidence interval on a correlation coefficient, apply no multiple-testing correction, and run no two-group test, so a set of per-strategy coefficients is reported as its range and its median and a named group is reported by its median and median absolute deviation beside the others. Analyses ran under Python 3.11 with pandas 2.3.3, numpy 1.26.4, scipy 1.12.0, scikit-learn 1.3.2, statsmodels 0.14.1, matplotlib 3.10.8 and seaborn 0.13.2. Every number quoted below is produced by `analysis/article2_generalizability.py` from the pinned metric snapshot `metrics_comprehensive_260905.csv`, the canonical-set definition in Supplementary File 3 of the source benchmark and the per-fold table `prediction_folds_long.csv`, and is written to the nine tables of Supplementary Files 1 and 2.

## Results and discussion

### Metric classes capture non-overlapping components of harmonization quality

We began from the two metric families that dominate the published comparisons, the global PCA-based family and the local neighbourhood family, and asked how they order the same 2,234 approaches. As the global representative we took PCReg, reported throughout in polarity-adjusted form, so that 1 means that the annotation column explains none of the PCA variation and 0 means that it explains all of it. Plotting PCReg for the biology column against PCReg for the batch column gave a weak negative association across the whole set (Spearman r = -0.159, p = 3.7 x 10^-14^, Figure 1A and 1B). The 15 clustermap-selected approaches occupied the upper right of that plane, with a batch median of 0.871 (median absolute deviation, MAD, 0.122) against 0.626 for the remaining approaches and a biology median of 0.820 (MAD 0.063) against 0.802. We carry that group through the whole analysis as one named group beside the strategies, imputations and methods, and quote its medians without a two-group test against the remainder.

Individual methods behaved in ways a single global number cannot express. 33_amdbnorm returned a batch PCReg of exactly 1.0000 in each of its 14 completed runs, and 13_fsmvn reached a median of 1.0000 across a range from 0.9967 upwards, yet neither was among the clustermap selections outside the no-removal strategy (Figure 1A). 05_combat and 29_combat_ref separated biology better than the remaining methods across the A, B, I, J and E1 to E3 strategies (median biology PCReg 0.568 against 0.778, full range 0.357 to 0.823) without being selected either. 14_qsmooth was last on batch correction, with a median of 0.208 and MAD 0.098. Among strategies, malignant-only, FFPE-only and no-removal were weakest on biology, with medians of 0.939 (malignant-only), 0.925 (FFPE-only) and 0.883 (no removal), and RNA-seq-only was strongest on batch, with a median of 0.872 (Figure 1B). The malignant-only and FFPE-only strategies contain no normal B cells, so their biology column carries four classes in all malignant-only runs and in all but one of the 164 FFPE-only runs, which is part of why their biology PCReg is high.

Decomposing the variance by component showed where the batch signal goes. Five methods (15_fsqn_py, 17_quantile, 18_rank, 20_shambhala and 25_angel) spread the variance slowly across components, starting at 17.3 to 25.3% on the first and 11.3 to 15.3% on the second, eight further methods and the clustermap group concentrated 26.2 to 43.3% on the first, and the remaining 18 methods placed up to 79.9% on the first component (11_harmony) and 79.5% (27_dwd) (Figure 1C). For the five leading methods by mean batch PCReg (03_limma, 07_pycombat, 13_fsmvn, 28_npn and 33_amdbnorm) the batch-explained R^2^ stayed below 1% at every one of the first ten components, with a maximum of 0.0024 at the second component of 07_pycombat. Without harmonization and without batch removal the same dataset gives a batch PCReg of 0.226, which means that batch accounts for 77.4% of the PCA variance summed over all components.

The local family ordered the approaches differently. Taking the local inverse Simpson's index as its representative, 10_mnn led on batch mixing with a median of 1.72 and MAD 0.209, against 1.16 and MAD 0.103 over all approaches, and 14_qsmooth came last at 1.07 and MAD 0.032 (Figure 2A). Batch and biology mixing rose together across the whole set (Spearman r = 0.527, p = 7 x 10^-160^), and the association held inside every strategy except the Affymetrix-only one, with r between 0.374 and 0.815 at a median of 0.617. The tSNE entropy of the batch labels agreed with that ordering, placing 10_mnn at a median of 0.179 with MAD 0.025 and 14_qsmooth at 0.0178 with MAD 0.0086 (Figure 2C), whereas tSNE centroid dispersion, a global quantity computed in the same embedding, split the methods into a low group and a high group, with method means between 0.24 for 13_fsmvn and 1.00 for 27_dwd, and placed the clustermap group on the boundary between them (Figure 2D).

The third family, distributional convergence, separated the approaches on yet another axis. Comparing the mean within-batch KS D statistic over 1,000 randomly drawn genes against the mean between-batch D statistic for the same genes, the clustermap group fell in the lower half of the plane (median 0.445, MAD 0.041), individual methods formed vertical bands, and strategies formed horizontal layers with the RNA-seq-only strategy at the lower left (within-batch median 0.439, MAD 0.015; between-batch median 0.332, MAD 0.052; Figure 3A, 3B). Missing values and output scale separated the methods again: 18 of the 31 methods lost no sample to a missing value under any strategy, 9 lost 0.003 to 1.4% of samples, and 4 (26_xpn, 20_shambhala, 15_fsqn_py, 17_quantile) lost 30.2 to 38.1% (Figure 3C). At gene level the same 18 lost nothing, 10 lost 0.004 to 2.41% of genes, 15_fsqn_py lost 13.0% and 17_quantile 16.2%, and 21_harmonizr lost all genes under 8 strategies of the 14 (Figure 3D). 06_combat_seq and 08_inmoose_combatseq returned mean maximum expression values of 2.76 x 10^8^ and 1.46 x 10^11^, against 99th percentiles of 18.5 and 17.9, 20_shambhala returned a mean median expression value of 1,359.6 and a mean maximum of 555,004.7, and 19 of the 31 methods returned a mean minimum below zero, with 13_fsmvn lowest at -40.6 (Figure 3E). Across the 15 clustermap approaches the median expression ran from 2.63 to 6.70, and the maximum from 12.6 to 28.6 (Figure 3F).

The three families ordered the same set of approaches differently. The rest of this article makes that observation quantitative.

### Global variance-based metrics saturate below the threshold of biological recovery

Twelve of the 31 methods overlapped batches better than biology in PCA space, and five of them (03_limma, 07_pycombat, 13_fsmvn, 28_npn and 33_amdbnorm) reached a mean batch PCReg at or above 0.999, with 33_amdbnorm at exactly 1.0000 (Supplementary Figure 1A). At strategy level only two of the 14 strategies, RNA-seq-only and Affymetrix-only, overlapped batches better than biology (Supplementary Figure 1B), which places the choice of method above the choice of strategy at this level of resolution.

Saturation is what makes these values hard to read. Of the 198 approaches whose batch PCReg exceeded 0.9999, generated by six methods (03_limma, 13_fsmvn, 07_pycombat, 21_harmonizr, 33_amdbnorm and 28_npn), 98.5% returned a kBET acceptance rate under 0.1 (Supplementary Figure 6E). A global metric sitting at its theoretical optimum therefore carries two readings at once: the batches may have been aligned, or the structure that was to be aligned may have been flattened.

The remaining global metrics reproduced PCReg rather closely and added little separation. DSC for the batch column followed PCReg along a monotone sigmoid (Supplementary Figure 3C), and DSC for the platform column followed DSC for the batch column linearly on a logarithmic scale (Supplementary Figure 3D). All 15 clustermap approaches fell below the overall median batch DSC of 0.962, although the four MNN approaches (0.781 to 0.957) sat at the boundary, about two orders of magnitude higher than the FSQN R approaches (0.0013 to 0.0098). That follows from the definition of DSC as between-group scatter divided by within-group scatter, a ratio that stays sensitive to outliers after log transformation. Average silhouette width tracked PCReg noisily, with the Affymetrix-only and FFPE-only strategies worst (means of 0.165 and 0.078, against a range of -0.066 to -0.019 elsewhere, Supplementary Figure 3I) and 02_median_scaling and 33_amdbnorm best (-0.286 and -0.276, Supplementary Figure 3J).

The two lower-ranked pipeline factors behaved as the source benchmark reports [38], and we quantify them here on the global metric. Softimpute exceeded k-nearest-neighbour imputation by between 1.7 and 9.7 percentage points, with a mean of 4.8, across the 11 methods with the lowest batch PCReg, while strict exclusion and k-nearest-neighbour imputation differed by 0.020 in mean batch PCReg over the affected methods (0.370 against 0.390, Supplementary Figure 2A). Post-removal raised the mean batch PCReg for 30 of the 31 methods, with a median shift of 0.030; 22_tmm rose furthest, from 0.70 up to 0.88, and 23_vst from 0.70 to 0.83. 12_scanorama was the one method whose metric fell, from 0.49 to 0.48 (Supplementary Figure 2C). Strategy composition moved the same metric further than either: every method reached at least 0.76 under the RNA-seq-only strategy, 0.72 under Affymetrix-only and 0.46 under Affymetrix-extended (Supplementary Figure 2B), and 16_fsqn_r fell from a cross-strategy mean of 0.83 to 0.30 under microarray-only and 0.08 under FFPE-only, which is the arithmetic form of the observation that feature-specific quantile normalization does not transfer to FFPE material.

### Local neighbourhood metrics discriminate between approaches but scale with class composition

Only six of the 31 methods (02_median_scaling, 10_mnn, 16_fsqn_r, 25_angel, 28_npn and 33_amdbnorm) mixed batches better than biology by LISI (Supplementary Figure 4A). The kBET acceptance rate was more severe still: it stayed under 0.01 for 82.0% of the 2,234 approaches and under 0.10 for 97.0% of them (Supplementary Figure 5A). Five of the 15 clustermap approaches exceeded 0.10, all of them SVA under the RNA-seq-only and FFPE-only strategies. Eight methods exceeded the unharmonized baseline of 0.027: 22_tmm at 0.331, 23_vst at 0.230, 10_mnn at 0.044, 04_sva at 0.036, 36_explobatch at 0.036, 13_fsmvn at 0.035, 20_shambhala at 0.029 and 19_tdm at 0.028. The other 22 methods scored below no harmonization at all (Supplementary Figure 5C).

Local values depend on how many classes the strategy leaves in the data, which limits comparison across strategies. The malignant-only and FFPE-only strategies retain four malignant groups and returned the lowest biology LISI (medians 1.10 and 1.07), whereas the FF-only strategy keeps every normal B-cell type and returned the highest value (median 1.62, Supplementary Figure 4B). The same two strategies returned the lowest biology graph connectivity (0.646 and 0.727 against 0.799 to 0.854 for the rest, Supplementary Figure 7D), although fewer groups should raise connectivity, which points at their assembly from heterogeneous FFPE batches. The RNA-seq-only strategy produced a tight group of 41 approaches spanning 18 methods, with kBET between 0.654 and 0.867, and none of them was selected by the clustermap, because their LISI values stayed between 1.24 and 1.38 (Supplementary Figure 5B). Reaching the maximum of one local metric is therefore not sufficient for selection either.

### Batch-associated variance is redistributed across principal components instead of being eliminated

Ten of the 14 strategies, and 19 of the 31 methods, placed a lower batch-explained R^2^ on the first principal component than on some later one (Figure 1C, Supplementary Figure 2F). Among the five methods whose explained variance decayed most slowly, four (all but 20_shambhala) reached their maximum batch R^2^ away from the first component. The clustermap group averaged 37.9% and 11.6% of total variance and 22.9% and 15.2% batch R^2^ on the first two components; 14_qsmooth, weakest on the PCA metrics, averaged 53.7% and 13.5% of variance with 90.8% and 67.1% batch R^2^; the unharmonized data averaged 73.5% and 9.7% of variance with 64.8% and 65.9% batch R^2^. The RNA-seq-only strategy and the clustermap group carried the lowest first-component variance (30.4% and 37.9%) and the smoothest decay, and the Affymetrix-only strategy the highest (66.0%), which its pre-quantile-normalized constituent cohort accounts for. Any summary that reads only the first component, or a metric aggregated over it, therefore overstates how much batch structure a method has removed.

### Distributional convergence is the only axis on which the harshest transformations rank first

On the fraction of cohort pairs whose expression distributions still differ by the KS test, 29 of the 31 methods scored below the unharmonized baseline of 0.948, and only 27_dwd was worse (0.965, Supplementary Figure 7A). Only ten methods brought the metric below 0.7, ranging from 0.173 for 28_npn and 0.217 for 26_xpn up to 0.691 for 03_limma, and the clustermap group reached 0.560, so 56.0% of cohort pairs still differed after the best available harmonization. Distance ratio separated the clustermap group as well (batch distance ratio between 0.845 and 1.032, with a median of 0.914 against 0.757 over the full set, Supplementary Figure 7E), with 27_dwd (0.508) and 14_qsmooth (0.531) below no harmonization (0.566). The Watermelon score did not separate that group at all (Supplementary Figure 7G to 7J), which makes it a candidate for an independent metric class on the same footing as the global and local families.

Grouping the methods by their published harshness tier makes the direction of the trade-off explicit. Low-harshness methods gave the highest batch PCReg under 11 strategies of 14, and the highest batch LISI under 10 of 14, while high-harshness methods came last on tSNE entropy under 12 strategies of 14 (Supplementary Figure 8A, 8C, 8D). High harshness prevailed on one axis only, distributional convergence, where it gave the lowest KS fraction significant under 13 strategies of 14 (Supplementary Figure 8F). Matching expression distributions is what the harshest transformations are good at, and it is also the property that a distribution-based metric cannot distinguish from a genuine alignment.

The equally weighted composite of all 87 metrics ranked the clustermap group first at 0.602, with the RNA-seq-only strategy next at 0.578 (Supplementary Figure 9A). Its arithmetic is dominated by a group that contributes little of its own separation: global distance metrics supply 47 of the 87 columns and 0.360 of the group's score while varying by only 0.017 across strategies and 0.030 across methods, whereas local neighbourhood metrics supply 22 columns and 0.109 of the score yet produce the largest between-group spreads, 0.043 across strategies and 0.049 across methods for the neighbour-based integration group (Supplementary Figure 9C, 9D). Batch-annotated columns supply 57 metrics of the 87 and between 60.7 and 67.4% of the composite, and the 18 biology columns separate approaches weakly: across strategies their spread is 0.053, and for the batch columns it is 0.122 (Supplementary Figure 9E, 9F). Sorting all approaches by the composite leaves an upper tail above 0.6 that holds 150 approaches of the 2,234 (6.7%), containing 9 of the 15 clustermap selections (Supplementary Figure 10). We quote no ordering of the harshness tiers by this composite: recomputation of that ordering from the deposited files returns the opposite direction to the published one and the exact construction is not recoverable, so the question is left open and no claim rests on it.

The harshness tiers can be compared on the raw biology-facing columns instead, with no composite involved, and there the tiers separate cleanly (Supplementary Figure 12). The tier membership is 344 low, 1,042 medium and 848 high approaches, but each test drops rows missing its own metric, so the tier medians below are quoted with the denominator of their own metric. Low-harshness methods held the mean marker correlation at 0.998 against 0.880 for medium and 0.922 for high (Kruskal-Wallis H = 340.1, p = 1.4 x 10^-74^, n = 344 / 1,042 / 848), and had the smallest marker-minus-housekeeping contrast (-0.000 against 0.009 and 0.031; H = 139.3, p = 5.5 x 10^-31^, n = 344 / 1,042 / 800). High-harshness methods destroyed cross-batch rank agreement (0.507 against 0.691 and 0.701; H = 287.1, p = 4.5 x 10^-63^, n = 344 / 1,042 / 847) and carried the smallest margin (0.030 against 0.054 and 0.045; H = 132.9, p = 1.4 x 10^-29^, same n). On the full-cut LOBO three-class F1 the low tier was last (0.631 against 0.712 and 0.715; H = 51.6, p = 6.1 x 10^-12^, n = 344 / 1,042 / 847); on the multiclass-only cut the three tiers spanned 0.021 (0.657, 0.678, 0.678; H = 11.8, p = 2.8 x 10^-3^, n = 333 / 1,016 / 825); and the two-class AUC did not separate them (0.901, 0.904, 0.904; H = 1.5, p = 0.48, same n). An earlier calculation of the multiclass-only test over the unrestricted fold table gave H = 11.2 and p = 0.0037, and an earlier reading of the medium-tier full-cut median gave 0.713; the values quoted here, H = 11.8, p = 2.8 x 10^-3^ and 0.712, come from the fold population restricted to the analysis set and from the table at three decimals, and the tier ordering is the same under both.

### Preservation of biomarker expression and of cross-batch rank structure elects a distinct set of approaches

None of the 87 metrics above reads the expression of a gene that anyone would act on. We therefore added two classes that do. Class L asks whether the expression of each marker-panel gene within a cohort survives harmonization, and class M asks whether the panel keeps the same rank order between batches of the same biological group while losing it between batches of different groups. Plotting raw against harmonized expression, gene by gene and cohort by cohort, shows what each class is reacting to: the unharmonized baseline lies on the identity line by construction (Figure 4A), 13_fsmvn moves every point along a per-gene affine path and keeps the within-cohort order (Figure 4B), 12_scanorama returns a cloud with no relation to the input (Figure 4C), and 29_combat_ref shifts the cohorts onto a common scale while keeping the within-cohort spread (Figure 4D).

Class L is saturated at the top of its range. Five methods returned a median within-cohort marker correlation of exactly 1.0000 (01_raw, 36_explobatch, 31_ruv3prps, 27_dwd, 21_harmonizr), and the low-harshness tier sat at 0.998. When class L elects on its own it therefore chooses unharmonized output. Of its 112 elected approaches, 55 are 01_raw and 38 are 36_explobatch, so 93 of 112 places go to output that is unchanged or close to unchanged, with 13_fsmvn and 02_median_scaling taking 7 each, 04_sva 4 and 03_limma 1 (Supplementary File 1, sheet `Method_census_by_class`). A marker correlation above 0.75 is therefore a gate that an approach must pass and cannot win on, and we treat it as such throughout.

Class M has the opposite failure. Its own composite is dominated by same-biology agreement, which rises whenever an algorithm makes all profiles alike, so class M elected only two methods: 10_mnn took 61 of its 112 places and 12_scanorama took 51. The median within-cohort marker correlation of those two methods is -0.0007 and -0.0082, and their median same-minus-different-biology margin is 0.0000, with a median change of -0.0489 against their own unharmonized baseline. An approach that collapses the panel onto a single profile maximizes agreement and carries no biology at all. The discriminative quantity is the margin, and the margin relative to the unharmonized baseline at the same strategy and imputation separates an approach that sharpened the biology from one that erased it.

Applying the two classes together as a gate, 1,686 of the 2,234 approaches (75.5%) reached a marker correlation above 0.75 with a positive margin. The methods that pass most often are 06_combat_seq (83 of its approaches), 03_limma (82), 07_pycombat (82), 08_inmoose_combatseq (82), 01_raw (80) and 05_combat (80), and by strategy the RNA-seq-only, FF-only and no-removal strategies pass most often (141, 130 and 130) with FFPE-only last (76 of 164). 01_raw passes the joint gate in 80 of its 84 approaches, so passing the gate does not require having harmonized anything.

Compared against each other on the two class columns, the batch-removal strategies spanned 0.0647 in median marker correlation, from malignant-only at 0.9791 and A_confirmed_bad at 0.9753 down to Affymetrix-extended at 0.9145 and rare-batches-removed at 0.9144, and they spanned 0.0667 in median margin, from Affymetrix-only at 0.0757 down to FFPE-only at 0.0090. Only two strategies raised the margin above their own unharmonized baseline in the median, FFPE-only (+0.0180) and RNA-seq-only (+0.0077). Imputations separated far less: median marker correlation was 0.9586 under k-nearest-neighbour imputation, 0.9471 under strict exclusion and 0.9343 under softimpute, a range of 0.0243 against the 0.0647 spanned by strategies, and the median margin ran 0.0513, 0.0432 and 0.0364 in the reverse order (Figure 4H). Methods separated most: median marker correlation ran from 1.0000 for five methods to -0.0082 for 12_scanorama, and median margin from 0.1263 for 05_combat to 0.0000 for 10_mnn and 12_scanorama (Figure 4E, 4F).

Requiring the two directions of class M separately leaves few approaches. Only 61 of the 2,150 non-raw approaches (2.8%) raised same-biology agreement and lowered different-biology agreement against their own baseline, which we call agreement-specific (Figure 4G). They concentrate in the FFPE-only strategy (21 of 61), microarray-only (11) and rare-batches-removed (7), spread across imputations (24 under k-nearest-neighbour, 21 under softimpute, 16 under strict exclusion) and are led by 05_combat (14), then 36_explobatch, 06_combat_seq and 08_inmoose_combatseq (6 each), 14_qsmooth and 25_angel (5 each) and 04_sva and 16_fsqn_r (4 each). The two highest by margin gain are an exact tie, FFPE-only with k-nearest-neighbour imputation and no post-removal, under 06_combat_seq and under 08_inmoose_combatseq: both return a margin of 0.439161 and a gain over their own baseline of +0.457154, made of +0.257 in same-biology agreement and -0.200 in different-biology agreement. An earlier reading of the same ranking named 06_combat_seq alone as the top approach; the two implementations of ComBat-Seq agree to six decimals here, so neither may be quoted without the other, and their agreement is a positive control on the class M computation.

The two methods that produce these top margins are also the two that returned mean maximum expression values of 2.76 x 10^8^ and 1.46 x 10^11^ (Figure 3E). They win on classes L and M while failing a basic check on the scale of their own output. We report that contradiction because the scale of the output lies outside what classes L and M measure: a class elects what it measures.

Marker readouts are known to move with the preparation of the input material, including extracellular-vesicle and plasma-derived inputs [50,51], which is the same axis that the FF-only and FFPE-only strategies separate here. Shambhala-2 sits in mid-field on every axis of this section. Across its 84 approaches the median marker correlation is 0.840 and the median margin is 0.0048, and none of its approaches enters the agreement-specific group of 61. Its prediction values are given with both fold cuts in the next section.

### Cross-batch prediction elects a third set, whose ranking is stable across two cuts of the fold population

Class N asks whether a classifier fitted on the batches that are present can label the samples of a batch it has never seen. Across the analysis set this produced 32,746 three-class folds over 24 RNA batches. Of those, 15,436 (47.1%) contained a single biological class and reached a median macro F1 of 0.903, against 0.7334 over the 17,310 folds carrying two or more classes; inside the latter, two-class folds were the harder ones (median 0.629) and three-class folds the easier (median 0.804) (Figure 5C).

The fold population has to be restricted to the approaches under analysis before it is counted. The per-fold table holds 53,737 three-class folds over 3,738 run identifiers under 50 method names, and a census over all of them describes approaches this article never analyses: 20,991 of those folds fall outside the analysis set, of which 20,343 belong to the 17 non-canonical Shambhala parameter variants and the remaining 648 to 34_arsyn and 38_harman, the two methods whose approaches all fail the 5% all-missing criterion. The canonical Shambhala variant is retained and contributes 1,200 folds. An earlier count over the unrestricted table gave 53,737 folds with 25,426 single-class (47.3%) and fold-level medians of 0.952, 0.648 and 0.830 by class count. The counts quoted here come from the restricted population, and the conclusion is the same under both: about half of the held-out batches carry one class, and they score higher than the folds that require the classifier to separate classes.

The two cuts agree closely and do not coincide. Spearman correlation between the full-cut mean and the multiclass-only mean is 0.810 over the 2,174 approaches carrying both, and 0.815 over the 2,058 with at least three multiclass folds; an earlier value of 0.812 came from the unrestricted fold table. That is high enough for the two cuts to select the same methods and low enough for the order inside the leading group to change, which is why both are reported everywhere below.

Two fold structures make a high value uninformative, and we report the approaches that carry them with the structure stated beside the number. Fifty-nine of the 2,234 approaches have no multiclass fold at all, and 20 Affymetrix-only approaches with post-removal report a full-cut F1 of 1.000 with a perfect worst fold and zero multiclass folds (Figure 5C). Separately, the strategy that retains the known-bad batches gives the highest prediction values anywhere: A_confirmed_bad with strict exclusion under 29_combat_ref and post-removal reaches a full-cut F1 of 0.958, a multiclass-only mean of 0.924, a worst multiclass fold of 0.643 over 11 multiclass folds, a two-class AUC of 0.998 and a marker correlation of 0.957. If a retained bad batch is also diagnosis-pure, held-out prediction measures that confounding, so this approach is reported and is not offered as a recommendation. Protecting the biological covariate during correction does not remove the problem: when groups are unevenly distributed across batches, the corrected data are not batch-effect free and the estimation errors are deflated [52].

Excluding the two bad-batch-retaining strategies and approaches with fewer than five multiclass folds leaves 1,527 eligible approaches, and their leading approaches are given in Table 1 with both cuts side by side. The leading non-confounded approach is no removal with softimpute under 29_combat_ref without post-removal: full-cut mean F1 0.946, multiclass-only mean 0.887, worst multiclass fold 0.462 over 11 multiclass folds, two-class AUC 0.932, marker correlation 0.880. Its per-batch detail shows where the two cuts come from (Figure 5B): 13 of its 24 folds are single-class and score between 0.986 and 1.000, and the three hardest multiclass folds are GPL96_FFPE_Unknown (n = 84, 2 classes, F1 0.462, AUC 0.543), RNASeq_FFPE_PolyA (n = 35, 2 classes, F1 0.698, AUC 0.874) and RNASeq_FF_PolyA (n = 1,039, 3 classes, F1 0.718, AUC 0.972). An earlier reading of the same approach counted 10 single-class folds and named only two hard folds; the two named folds and their values reproduce exactly, the single-class count and the identity of the second-hardest fold do not, and the values here are read from the deposited per-fold table.

Combining all six prediction and preservation columns into one rank-percentile index reproduces the same warning about fold structure. The 15 highest values over the whole analysis set, from 0.933 to 0.982, all belong to Affymetrix-only approaches with post-removal and zero multiclass folds, because a missing column raises a mean of the columns that remain. Restricted to the 1,527 eligible approaches, the index is led by no removal with strict exclusion under 29_combat_ref with post-removal at 0.8875, which carries a full-cut F1 of 0.935, a multiclass-only mean of 0.866, a worst multiclass fold of 0.541 over 11 folds, a two-class AUC of 0.980 and a marker correlation of 0.953 (Figure 5D). The 15 clustermap-selected approaches rank between 323rd and 2,038th of 2,234 on the same index.

### The sets elected by the individual metric classes show limited intersection

Running the election once per class over the same 2,234 candidates gives eight sets of 112 approaches and one published set of 15. The largest agreement between any two of them is between the local class and the equally weighted composite, Jaccard 0.333, which is 56 shared approaches (Figure 5E, Supplementary File 2, sheet `Cross_election_matrix`). The composite's other overlaps follow from its construction as the mean of four classes: 0.325 with the distributional class (55 approaches), 0.295 with the structural class (51) and 0.217 with the global class (40). Between the four component classes themselves the agreement is lower: local and distributional share 51 approaches (Jaccard 0.295), global and distributional 27 (0.137), local and structural 17 (0.082), global and local 15 (0.072), global and structural 15 (0.072), and distributional and structural 12 (0.057). No pair of metric classes agrees on even a third of its choices.

The three biology-facing classes sit almost entirely outside that structure. Classes L and M share no approach, classes M and N share no approach, and classes L and N share one (Jaccard 0.0045). Class N shares nothing with the global class and nothing with the composite, and its largest overlap with any other class is 10 approaches with the structural class (0.047). Class M reaches 12 approaches with the local class (0.057) and 9 with the structural class (0.042). Class L reaches 9 approaches with the composite (0.042) and 7 with the distributional class (0.032).

The 15 approaches that the published clustermap selected share 9 approaches with the local class (Jaccard 0.076), 6 with the distributional class (0.050), 4 each with the global class and the composite (0.033), and 3 each with the structural class and class M (0.024). They share none with class L and none with class N. The set that a joint reading of 87 metrics produced therefore has no member in common with the set that cross-batch prediction elects, and none with the set that marker preservation elects.

The method censuses say why (Figure 5F, Figure 5G). The global class puts 86 of its 112 places into two methods, 13_fsmvn (59) and 03_limma (27), both of which drive batch PCReg to its ceiling. Class L puts 93 places into 01_raw and 36_explobatch. Class M puts all 112 into 10_mnn and 12_scanorama. Class N puts 84 into four ComBat implementations. The local, distributional, structural and composite classes spread over 24, 22, 20 and 24 methods respectively, and share their leading method, 16_fsqn_r, which takes 9, 12, 18 and 18 places. Four of the nine groups are therefore single-method verdicts, and they name four different methods.

This is the cost of choosing a criterion before running a benchmark. A study that reports global metrics would recommend 13_fsmvn, whose median full-cut LOBO F1 is 0.537 and whose median multiclass-only F1 is 0.624, both in the bottom five methods. A study that reports cross-batch rank agreement without the margin would recommend 10_mnn, whose median within-cohort marker correlation is -0.0007. A study that reports LOBO prediction alone would recommend ComBat-Seq implementations that return mean maximum expression values of 2.76 x 10^8^ and 1.46 x 10^11^. Each recommendation is correct under its own criterion and fails under the next one. The choice of metric class therefore determines the recommendation more than the data do.

The practical consequence is that a benchmark has to state its metric classes, its gates and its held-out test before it ranks anything, and has to report all of them afterwards. Class L is a gate that an approach must pass and cannot win on, because a per-batch monotone transform scores near 1.0 by construction and the low-harshness tier sits at 0.998 while predicting worst of the three tiers (0.631 on the full cut). Class M has to be read as a margin against the unharmonized baseline at the same strategy and imputation, because agreement alone rewards collapse. Class N has to be cut by batch and reported on both fold cuts, because 47.1% of the held-out batches carry one class and score a median of 0.903 against 0.7334 for the rest, and because a confounded strategy reaches the highest value of all (0.958 full cut, 0.924 multiclass-only). We have drawn these three traps and the six steps around them as a recipe (Figure 6), in the same terms the analysis above used.

## Conclusions

We ran the same election eight times over one set of 2,234 harmonization approaches, changing only the metric class that was allowed to vote, and the winners changed with it. The largest agreement between any two elected sets of 112 approaches was 56 approaches (Jaccard 0.333), and that pair was a class and the composite built from it. Between the three biology-facing classes the agreement was one approach in total: marker preservation and cross-batch rank agreement shared none, cross-batch rank agreement and prediction shared none, and marker preservation and prediction shared one. The 15 approaches selected by a joint reading of 87 metrics shared none of the 112 that prediction elected.

Each class elected what it was built to reward. Marker preservation elected unharmonized and near-unharmonized output, because a per-batch monotone transform leaves within-cohort ranks unchanged and scores near 1.0 by construction. Cross-batch rank agreement elected the two methods that make all profiles alike, whose median within-cohort marker correlation is -0.0007 and -0.0082. Global variance metrics elected the two methods that drive batch PCReg to its ceiling, one of which ranks in the bottom five on prediction. Cross-batch prediction elected four implementations of ComBat, two of which return mean maximum expression values of 2.76 x 10^8^ and 1.46 x 10^11^. Every one of these verdicts is defensible inside its own class and indefensible outside it.

Three properties of the metrics themselves caused most of that behaviour, and all three can be handled by reporting. A within-cohort correlation metric saturates, so it works as a gate at a stated threshold and does not order the approaches that pass: the low-harshness tier holds it at 0.998 while returning the lowest full-cut prediction F1 of the three tiers (0.631). An agreement metric rises whenever an algorithm converges profiles, so it has to be read as a margin between same-biology and different-biology pairs, taken against the unharmonized baseline at the same strategy and imputation; requiring both directions of that margin leaves 61 of 2,150 non-raw approaches (2.8%). A prediction metric depends on the composition of the held-out batch, so it has to be cut by batch and reported on both fold cuts: 15,436 of the 32,746 three-class folds carry one class and reach a median macro F1 of 0.903 against 0.7334 for the rest, and the highest prediction values anywhere come from a strategy that keeps the known-bad batches (full-cut F1 0.958, multiclass-only 0.924).

We set these out as a recipe (Figure 6). Declare the metric classes before computing them. Compute every class over the full approach set instead of a shortlist. Use the within-cohort correlation as a gate at a stated threshold. Rank on a margin taken against an explicit unharmonized baseline. Validate with leave-one-batch-out prediction whose projection is fitted on training batches only, reported on both fold cuts with the multiclass fold count beside each value. Report the elected set of every class together with their intersections, so a reader can see which recommendation depends on which criterion. Applied to this dataset the recipe leaves a small set of approaches that pass every step, led by no removal with softimpute under 29_combat_ref, which reaches a full-cut F1 of 0.946, a multiclass-only mean of 0.887 over 11 multiclass folds and a marker correlation of 0.880.

The limits of this work follow from its design. All 2,234 approaches come from one dataset of germinal-centre B-cell lymphomas, so the numbers are specific to a setting in which the biological contrast is small and the technical contrast is large. The election threshold of 5% is a choice, and a different threshold changes the set sizes although not the ordering of the overlaps. Classes L and M rest on one 633-gene panel, and a different panel would move their members. The prediction class uses three diagnostic classes and 24 batches, so its folds are coarse. What does not depend on these choices is the finding that the classes disagree, because that disagreement is measured between sets drawn from the same 2,234 candidates by the same procedure.
